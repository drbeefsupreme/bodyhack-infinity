# Conditioning and leanness terms

Research notes for the Bodybuilding Appearance Explorer.

**Scope.** Conditioning as a judging criterion, and the terms *lean*, *cut*, *shredded*, *peeled*, *diced*, *ripped*, *crispy*, *grainy*, *stage-ready*, *in shape*, and *off-season / soft*. The sibling fullness research file covers *dry*, *full*, *flat*, *spilled over*, and the water-versus-fat claims. This file points to it where the terms meet.

**Conventions.** All URLs were fetched on 2026-10-09. Citations look like [S12] (see "Sources"). **[INFERENCE]** marks my own reasoning, not a statement in a source. This file gives no dehydration, diuretic, drug, or contest-prep protocol.

## Part A — Cross-term findings

### A1. What the federation rules actually say

| Federation and document | Words used | Short quotes |
|---|---|---|
| IFBB (international), Men's Bodybuilding Rules, 2026 edition [S1] | "quality", "density, separation, definition", "condition" | Judges assess "proportion, symmetry, muscle size and quality (density, separation, definition) as well as skin tone" (Art. 7.1). They look for "muscular bulk, balanced development, muscular density and definition" (Art. 10.1). The Chief Judge may exclude a competitor for "too high bodyfat level" (Art. 10.1). Judges must remember that "a competitor may present a different condition in the Finals compared to the Prejudging" (Art. 14.1). |
| NPC and NPC Worldwide (amateur bodies linked to the IFBB Pro League) [S2][S3] | "muscularity", "condition" | Men's bodybuilding is scored on the "total package", "a balance of size, symmetry, and muscularity". Men's Physique wants "muscularity and overall condition", and "extreme muscularity will be marked down". Figure wants "a small degree of muscularity with separation but no visible striations" and "not excessively lean". Fit Model wants a "core that is in shape but not too conditioned or lean". |
| WNBF (drug-tested) [S4][S5] | "Conditioning/Leanness", "Muscularity" | "Conditioning/Leanness: Degree of body fat, revealing muscle definition and striations" [S5]. Bodybuilding muscles should be "fully separated, with striations and cross striations, with no muscles under-conditioned, and no muscles flat", with "the best combination of leanness and muscle size possible" [S5]. Round 2 is "Muscularity/Conditioning" [S5]. |
| OCB (drug-tested) [S6] | "Definition" | "Extreme leanness with deep cuts and striations in all muscle groups. An overall grainy look throughout the physique should be seen." Athletes "should not display deeply drawn-in faces, a stringy-looking muscularity (an emaciated look), or less shapely muscularity due to muscle glycogen depletion." |
| Natural Bodybuilding Australia [S7] | "condition" | Physique: "lean enough to show a full six pack … the beginnings of striations are acceptable". Bodybuilding: "full six pack, separation in shoulders, arms quads, hamstrings, glutes, with cross striations where achievable." |

**Industry vocabulary in a rulebook.** NPC Worldwide lists "ripped, shredded, peeled, striated, dry, diced, hard, vascular, grainy, massive, thick, dense" as "common terms used in the bodybuilding industry". It uses them to say what Women's Physique should *not* look like [S2].

**Symmetry and presentation.** IFBB favors "a harmonious, classical physique", "good posture and athletic bearing", and "good skin tone" (Art. 10.2) [S1]. WNBF says that bodybuilding posing is "only judged indirectly, insomuch as it impacts the appearance of symmetry and muscularity" [S5]. In a FLEX judges' column, judge Steve Weinberger describes the top end: "deep, clean separation between the individual muscles, and smaller lines, or striations, across the muscle belly itself"; body fat "at a minimum, but still the muscle itself should be full and round"; skin "tight and smooth with a light sheen that emphasizes the dark tan" [S8].

**Key point.** The rulebooks rarely define "conditioning". They list visible cues: separation, definition, density, hardness, striations, cross striations, vascularity, graininess, and skin tone. Of the documents I read, only WNBF ties the word to "degree of body fat" [S5]. WNBF also tells judges to describe what they see, not the cause: "need more separation and hardness in the legs" instead of "be more conditioned or dryer" [S5]. The app can follow the same rule: show cues, and label causes separately.

### A2. Is there an ordinal scale?

**Short answer.** Yes for visible cues across divisions. Only partly for the slang words.

1. **The rulebooks build a ladder of cues by division.**

| Level | Division anchor | Visible cues in the rule text |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | NPC Fit Model [S2] | "There should not be any separation showing in the shoulders, arms, or legs." Core "flat but not too detailed or separated". |
| 1 | WNBF Bikini [S5] | "defined abs, soft lines in legs"; "quads should be separated, but there should not be visible cross striations". |
| 2 | NPC Figure [S2]; WNBF Figure [S5] | "separation but no visible striations"; "no visible body fat"; should not have a "soft" look. |
| 3 | NBA Men's Physique [S7]; WNBF Fit Body [S5] | "full six pack"; separation in shoulders, arms, and legs; "the beginnings of striations"; not "shredded" to the bodybuilding extent. |
| 4 | Bodybuilding: WNBF, OCB, NBA [S5][S6][S7] | "deep cuts and striations in all muscle groups"; "cross striations"; striated glutes and hamstrings [S7][S9]; "an overall grainy look". |
| Ceiling | OCB [S6]; WNBF [S5] | Not "stringy", not "emaciated", not "flat". Fullness is still required. |

The ladder mixes men's and women's divisions to show the order of the cues. Contest body-fat levels differ by sex [S37].

2. **The slang words only partly form a scale.** The low and middle words have a rough order: *off-season/soft* < *lean* < *in shape* / *cut* < *stage-ready*. The top-end words have no stable order. Muscle & Fitness: "Ripped, peeled, diced, shredded, grainy: high-def bodybuilding has many words for the extra-lean. They all basically mean 'Holy $#%@!'" [S9]. BarBend puts *cut* below *shredded*: at the top level "they're all cut", but only some show "grainy and shrink-wrapped conditioning" [S11].
3. **Each top-end word carries a different picture, but the picture is not a rule.** *Peeled* points at the skin (nothing seems left over the muscle). *Shredded* points at strands (striations). *Diced* points at small blocks and crossing lines. *Ripped* is general high definition with muscle. *Grainy* is a fine texture. *Crispy* is a sharp, dry, hard overall look. [INFERENCE from the usage in S9–S28]
4. **Mainstream fitness uses a different, size-based scale.** Men's Health lists "cut, ripped, jacked, or swole" "on an ascending scale, from smallest to largest". It says "shredded" can describe the same physique as "cut" [S30]. So *shredded* means less in gym speech than on a bodybuilding stage.
5. **No credible source gives body-fat cut-offs for these words.** The app should not attach percentages to words.

### A3. Physiology: what the evidence supports

**Body fat at contest time.** Drug-free athletes unless noted. Small samples.

| Group | Start → contest | Method | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 men from 11 pooled case studies | 14.5% → 5.8%; all men below 10% | mixed | [S37] |
| 7 women (figure, bikini) from the same review | 21.3% → 13.1%; one woman below 10% | mixed | [S37] |
| 1 man, 12 months | 14.8% → 4.5%; 14.6% after recovery | as reported in abstract | [S38] |
| 1 man, 26 weeks | 17.5% → 7.4% | DXA | [S39] |
| 1 man, same day near contest | 9.6% (BodPod) vs 5.1% (DXA) | two methods | [S40] |
| 3 elite men | 9.1% → 5.0%; sum of 8 skinfolds 51.1 → 36.7 mm | 4-compartment model | [S41] |
| 5 women bodybuilders | 18.3% → 12.7%; sum of 8 skinfolds 76.3 → 50.8 mm | 4-compartment model | [S42] |
| 1 figure competitor | 20.3% → 12.2% | 4-compartment model | [S43] |
| 9 men, 16 weeks to 1 week out | fat mass 8.8 → 5.3 kg | as reported in abstract | [S44] |
| 1 enhanced Open pro (untested federation) | sum of 3 skinfolds 13.9 mm (off-season) vs 13.2 mm (10 days out) | skinfolds | [S51] |

**How to read these numbers.**
- Healthy men have a lower limit near 4–6% body fat, about 2.5 kg of fat, by DXA or underwater weighing. This comes from 55 soldiers on an 8-week training course, not from bodybuilders. Near that limit, subcutaneous fat losses became small [S45].
- In male bodybuilders, field methods (DXA, skinfolds, bioimpedance) had individual errors of 5.5–8 body-fat points, compared with a 4-compartment model [S46].
- Helms et al. describe contest level as "competition body fat levels (lacking any visible subcutaneous fat)" [S36]. Escalante et al. gloss "conditioning" as "low body fat levels" [S35].

**Subcutaneous fat (SAT) and skin.**
- Ultrasound in very lean elite athletes: two elite men (a world-champion swimmer and an international gymnast) had a mean SAT of only about 0.8–1.1 mm per site [S47]. SAT patterns differ between people and sites; a female gymnast's SAT sum rose 62% with a 1.4 kg weight gain during reduced training [S47].
- Drug-free physique athletes lose ultrasound-measured SAT during contest prep [S50].
- Full skin thickness is about 1.7 mm on the forearm and 1.9 mm on the leg; the epidermis is about 0.1 mm (healthy adults, not athletes) [S48]. Skin thickness differs with sex and age [S49].
- [INFERENCE] At contest leanness, the skin can be as thick as, or thicker than, the fat layer over many muscles. Differences in skin between people could then matter more. I found no study of skin thickness in bodybuilders.

**How SAT changes visible muscle borders.**
- I found **no study** that measures SAT thickness against judged or rated "definition" in bodybuilders.
- Indirect support comes from cosmetic surgery. High-definition liposculpture thins fat over and between muscles "to highlight the 3-dimensional muscular anatomy" [S54]. Abdominal etching uses differential liposuction "to enhance the detail of abdominal musculature" [S55]. Both rest on the idea that a thinner cover reveals grooves.
- Viewers judge fat and muscle on separate perceptual axes [S58]. Research groups now build 3D bodies that vary adiposity and muscularity separately [S57], and rate graded renders of abdominal definition (basic, moderate, extreme) [S56].

**Which cues come from low SAT, and which from other factors.**

| Visible cue | Role of low SAT | Other factors | Evidence level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grooves at muscle borders; abdominal blocks | Main driver [S36][S54][S55] | Muscle size and shape; flexing [S63]; light direction [S59][S60] | Indirect |
| Striations and cross striations | Required: rule texts tie them to extreme leanness [S5][S6] | Contraction; layout of fiber bundles [INFERENCE]; light angle | No direct evidence of mechanism |
| Visible veins (vascularity) | Clinical data: obesity and "venous invisibility" are both risk factors for difficult IV access [S67]. This fits the idea that fat hides veins [INFERENCE] | Local warming made IV insertion faster and more successful [S66]; heat raises skin blood flow up to 6–8 L/min [S65]; warmth, pump, and pose make veins fuller [INFERENCE] | Indirect |
| "Thin skin", "paper-thin" look | Very low SAT | Skin thickness varies between people [S48][S49]; no study shows that prep thins skin | Community claim |
| "Dry" versus "watery" | — | One n=1 case: ultrasound subcutaneous thickness fell 10% in the last 3 days [S52]; a review finds water and sodium practices not shown to be "specifically effective and/or safe" [S35] | Contested; see fullness file |
| Grainy texture | Required | Muscle size; surface relief of fiber bundles; light angle [INFERENCE] | No direct evidence |
| "Hard", "dense" | Helps | Contraction [S63]; muscle size | No direct evidence |
| Too far: "stringy", "flat" | — | OCB links "less shapely muscularity" to glycogen depletion [S6]; muscle thickness fell in an elite pro during the cut [S51] | Rule text; n=1 |

### A4. Presentation confounds

1. **Tan.** IFBB bans tans that wipe off [S1]. NPC requires "the appearance of a natural tan" and bans metallic bronzers [S2][S3]. OCB asks for "an evenly applied spray tan" so that judges can "best view the physique" [S6]. WNBF says an insufficient tan "can obscure key details of conditioning and symmetry" [S5]. I found no academic test of tan and perceived definition. [INFERENCE] Darker skin reflects less light, so bright stage light washes out less of the shading in grooves.
2. **Oil and sheen.** IFBB: "The excessive application of oil on the body is strictly prohibited; however, body oils and moisturizers may be used in moderation" [S1]. NPC has a similar rule [S2]. Judge Weinberger wants "a light sheen" [S8]. In vision science, specular highlights give strong cues to 3D shape [S61][S62]. [INFERENCE] A light sheen makes muscle curvature easier to read; heavy oil adds glare. I found no bodybuilding study.
3. **Stage lighting.** People read shape from shading as if one light source lights the scene [S59] and as if the light comes from above [S60]. [INFERENCE] Strong overhead stage light lights the top of each muscle belly and leaves the groove below in shadow. This deepens apparent separation, mainly on the abs, serratus, chest, and quads. WNBF moves competitors on stage "based on differences in stage lighting" [S5].
4. **Pose and flexing.** Muscle architecture and thickness change markedly with contraction, mostly at low to moderate effort [S63]. IFBB pose rules tell competitors to "contract as many other muscles as possible" [S1]. Condition also changes during the show day. IFBB warns of "a different condition in the Finals" [S1]. Coach Hany Rambod said that evening competitors who had eaten all day did not "look crispy" [S13].
5. **Camera.** Camera distance changes facial proportions in photos [S64]. [INFERENCE] The same lens geometry applies to bodies, so close wide-angle phone photos change apparent proportions. Social-media photos also use chosen light and a pump. I found no study of perceived conditioning in photos.

### A5. Safety note

Dehydration, diuretic, and drug practices exist in contest-prep culture. This file gives no instructions for them.
- A case report describes severe low potassium with paralysis after diuretic misuse combined with fluid restriction [S69].
- A peer-reviewed review reports that bodybuilders self-prescribe "potentially dangerous pharmaceutical diuretics" [S35].
- A study of 20,286 male IFBB competitors found 121 deaths, including 46 sudden cardiac deaths. Professionals had a higher risk than amateurs [S68].
- WNBF asks organizers to make sure competitors "are not dangerously dehydrated" [S5].

## Part B — Term sections

### 1. Conditioning (judging criterion)

1. **Meaning** — How much of the developed muscle a judge can see, read through definition cues. Most of it is leanness. WNBF: "Degree of body fat, revealing muscle definition and striations" [S5]. A peer-reviewed review: "'conditioning' (low body fat levels)" [S35]. Judges also want fullness, so conditioning is not leanness alone [S5][S8].
2. **Visible features** — Deep grooves between muscles and between heads of one muscle: pec–delt tie-in, delt heads, triceps, quad separations, hamstring–glute tie-in. Clear abdominal blocks and obliques. Striations and cross striations on the chest, delts, and quads. At the top end, striated glutes and hamstrings [S5][S6][S7][S9]. Visible veins. A "hard" look. Tight skin with a light sheen [S8].
3. **Confused neighbors** — *Muscularity* (size). *Fullness* (round muscle bellies; see fullness file). *Dry* (a claimed water cause). *Symmetry*. Federations file the cues under different labels: IFBB under "quality" [S1], NPC under "muscularity" [S2], OCB under "definition" [S6].
4. **Consistency of usage** — Mostly consistent as a concept. The labels differ by federation. Each division sets a different target [S2][S5][S6][S7].
5. **Causes: supported vs. speculative** — *Supported:* very low body fat at contest (pooled drug-free case studies: 5.8% in men in bodybuilding, 13.1% in women in figure and bikini) [S37]; SAT falls during prep [S50]; thinner fat over and between muscles reveals muscle landmarks (surgical evidence) [S54][S55]. *Plausible:* muscle size and shape set how deep the grooves can look [INFERENCE]; light, tan, sheen, and pose change the look (A4). *Speculative:* water shifts in the final days [S35][S52].
6. **Honest illustrative representation** — Use one "conditioning" control that (a) thins a smooth "cover" layer over the muscles, (b) deepens and sharpens grooves at anatomical borders, (c) adds striations only near the top of the range, and (d) keeps muscle volume and the skeleton fixed. Put tan, sheen, and stage light on separate "presentation" controls. Best regions: abs and obliques, quads, delts, pecs, the back in rear poses, and the glute–hamstring tie-in.
7. **Classification** — Distinct concept (judging criterion); visual description.

### 2. Lean

1. **Meaning** — Low body fat with a visible muscle outline. It is relative: "lean" in Bikini or Physique is far less extreme than in bodybuilding [S5][S7]. In science, "lean mass" is a body-composition measure (tissue that is not fat), not a look [S36][S44].
2. **Visible features** — An abdominal outline; NBA sets "a full six pack" as the Physique bar [S7]. Separation at the shoulders and arms. A flat waist. Few or no striations. Some forearm veins. WNBF Bikini: "softer than Figure but lean, with muscle separation" [S5].
3. **Confused neighbors** — *Toned* (bikini and figure language). *Cut* (more groove depth). *Shredded*. *Lean mass* (a science term). NPC Figure wants "not excessively lean", so *lean* can be too much for some divisions [S2].
4. **Consistency of usage** — Mostly consistent in direction (less fat). The level depends on the speaker and the division.
5. **Causes: supported vs. speculative** — *Supported:* less fat mass gives a thinner SAT layer [S47][S50]. *Supported:* SAT patterning differs between people and sites [S47]. *Plausible:* two people at the same body-fat percentage can show different abdominal detail [INFERENCE from S47]. *Speculative:* a fixed body-fat percentage for "lean"; I found no credible source.
6. **Honest illustrative representation** — Thin the cover layer a little. Show only the major borders: the abdominal midline and upper rows, the delt–arm line, and the outer quad line. Add no striations and few veins. Note in the UI that the order in which areas show detail differs between people.
7. **Classification** — Visual description (relative); overlapping slang.

### 3. Cut (and "cuts")

1. **Meaning** — Two senses. *Cuts* are the grooves between muscles, and *cut* means that they show. *Cutting* is also the diet phase that leads there [S10]. Shawn Ray on a pro: "The cuts are not there" [S28]. OCB asks for "deep cuts and striations" [S6].
2. **Visible features** — Clear grooves between muscles and between heads of one muscle: delt heads, the triceps outline, quad separations, abdominal rows, and serratus.
3. **Confused neighbors** — *Cutting* (process) versus *cut* (state). *Shredded* adds striations: BarBend says all top pros are "cut", but few are "most shredded" [S11]. Men's Health treats *cut* and *shredded* as the same in gym speech [S30]. The official word is *definition* [S1][S6].
4. **Consistency of usage** — Mostly consistent. The two senses (state and process) can confuse readers.
5. **Causes: supported vs. speculative** — *Supported:* fat loss reveals grooves; surgery that removes fat along the grooves adds detail [S54][S55]; fat loss during prep is large [S37]. *Plausible:* bigger muscle bellies make deeper valleys at the same fat level [INFERENCE]; flexing and overhead light deepen them [S63][S60]. *Untested claim:* "thick skin" hides the cuts (Shawn Ray) [S28].
6. **Honest illustrative representation** — Deepen and widen grooves at anatomical borders as soft valleys, not cracks. Leave muscle volume and the skeleton unchanged. Best regions: the quads (front), delts and triceps (side), abs (front), and lats and teres major (back).
7. **Classification** — Visual description; overlapping slang (state and process).

### 4. Shredded

1. **Meaning** — Very lean with very visible definition. On a bodybuilding stage it usually implies striations. Cambridge: "having strong, well-developed muscles that can be seen through the skin, and little body fat" [S31]. Writer John Romano: "shredded, i.e., extremely low body fat" [S25].
2. **Visible features** — Deep separations plus fine lines (striations) across the muscle bellies of the chest, delts, and quads. Detail in the hamstrings and glutes. Visible veins. BarBend: few pros "consistently display(ed) the striations to be deemed the most shredded" [S11]. WNBF tells Fit Body athletes not to look "shredded"; it names "striated glutes" and "a fully cross-striated physique with prominent vascularity" as that level [S5].
3. **Confused neighbors** — *Ripped* (Cambridge lists it as a synonym [S31]). *Peeled*, *diced*, *grainy*. *Cut* (gym speech treats them as equal [S30]).
4. **Consistency of usage** — Overlapping slang. Stage use is stricter than gym use [S11][S30].
5. **Causes: supported vs. speculative** — *Supported:* contest-level fat in men (about 5–6%) [S37], close to the healthy lower limit of about 4–6% [S45]. *No direct evidence found* for what forms visible striations. *Plausible:* bundles of muscle fibers show as surface ridges when the cover is very thin and the muscle is flexed [INFERENCE]. *Folklore:* dehydration "makes" a shredded look; evidence for water practices is lacking [S35] (see fullness file).
6. **Honest illustrative representation** — At the top of the conditioning range, add fine, low lines that follow fiber direction: a fan on the pec toward the upper arm, lines on each delt head, lines along the outer and inner quad, and diagonal lines on the glutes. Fade the lines when the muscle relaxes. Put veins on their own control.
7. **Classification** — Overlapping slang; visual description.

### 5. Peeled

1. **Meaning** — So lean that the skin seems peeled off the muscle. BarBend: "an extremely low level of body fat … the striations in muscle tissue are easily visible" [S10]. Romano: "Getting peeled is the only way to know what you're working with" [S25].
2. **Visible features** — Skin that looks very thin and tight over every muscle; fine detail everywhere; veins. Journalist Peter McGough called Andreas Münzer's skin texture "paper-thin" (as quoted by BarBend) [S12]. Muscle & Fitness on Flex Lewis in the rear double biceps: his body "seems to crack, revealing the fibers beneath the skin" [S9].
3. **Confused neighbors** — *Shredded*, *ripped*, *dry*, "thin skin". **Peeled versus shredded:** both are top-end words, and writers swap them [S9][S10]. In usage, *peeled* points at the cover (the skin seems gone), while *shredded* points at the muscle (split into strands) [INFERENCE]. A peeled look can cost fullness: Dennis James said one athlete was not going for "super-duper peeled" so that he could "stay fuller" [S26].
4. **Consistency of usage** — Overlapping slang. Mostly consistent in the meaning "extremely lean".
5. **Causes: supported vs. speculative** — *Supported:* SAT can be about 1 mm per site in very lean athletes [S47]. *Plausible:* at that point the skin (about 1.7–1.9 mm full thickness [S48]) is a large share of the cover, so people with thinner skin may look more peeled [INFERENCE]; skin thickness differs with sex and age [S49]. *Community claim:* "His skin is very thick. The cuts are not there" (Shawn Ray) [S28]. *No direct evidence found* that prep thins the skin. Water claims: see fullness file.
6. **Honest illustrative representation** — Take the cover layer to its minimum so the surface follows the muscle shape tightly, with sharper curvature at the borders. Keep normal skin color and pores. Do not label the control "thin skin". Label it "cover layer (fat + skin, schematic)". Best regions: abs, delts, arms, quads.
7. **Classification** — Overlapping slang; visual description; contested explanation (skin).

### 6. Diced

1. **Meaning** — Very lean, with the muscle surface cut into small blocks or crossing lines. Former pro Dennis James tied it to chest "cross striations … like diced" and "super deep fine lines" [S23]. Muscle & Fitness wrote that a wheelchair winner "showed up sliced and diced" [S24]. NPC lists "diced" as an industry term [S2].
2. **Visible features** — Two sets of lines that cross, along and across the muscle belly. Most often on the abs (blocks) and the chest (cross striations). Also on the quads: "zippered with cross-striations" (Muscle & Fitness on Darrem Charles) [S9].
3. **Confused neighbors** — *Shredded*, *striated*, the idiom "sliced and diced", *ripped*.
4. **Consistency of usage** — Overlapping slang. I found no formal definition.
5. **Causes: supported vs. speculative** — *Supported base:* the same very low SAT as *shredded* [S37][S47]. *No direct evidence found* for the cause of "cross striations". *Plausible:* crossing patterns come from muscle layout, such as tendinous bands across the rectus abdominis and fiber bundles at different angles in the chest [INFERENCE].
6. **Honest illustrative representation** — Add a crossing-line pattern only where the anatomy has crossing structures: the horizontal ab rows (tendinous intersections) with the vertical midline, and fine crossing lines on a flexed pec. Keep the lines low and tie them to the top of the conditioning range. Tag the pattern in the UI as slang.
7. **Classification** — Overlapping slang; visual description.

### 7. Ripped

1. **Meaning** — High muscle definition, usually with clear muscle size. Merriam-Webster: "having high muscle definition" [S32]. BarBend: "a way of describing a lifter's overall conditioning … low body fat … also packing a noticeable amount of muscle" [S10].
2. **Visible features** — Clear abs, separations in the shoulders and arms, and veins on the arms. Men's Health places *ripped* above *cut*, with "some mass, particularly in the arms and shoulders" [S30]. On a stage it overlaps with *shredded* [S9].
3. **Confused neighbors** — *Cut* (smaller in gym speech [S30]), *shredded*, *jacked* (size), *peeled*.
4. **Consistency of usage** — Mostly consistent in gym speech (lean plus muscular). Overlapping slang on stage [S9].
5. **Causes: supported vs. speculative** — Low fat mass plus muscle mass. No special mechanism is claimed, so there is nothing specific to test.
6. **Honest illustrative representation** — Combine a mid-to-high conditioning value with moderate muscle size. Show strong borders on the arms and shoulders and clear ab rows. Striations are optional. Best regions: arms, shoulders, abs.
7. **Classification** — Overlapping slang; visual description.

### 8. Crispy

1. **Meaning** — A newer spoken and social-media word of praise for a sharp, dry, hard top-end look. It appears in no rulebook. Merriam-Webster gives "crispy" only food and hair senses [S33].
2. **Visible features** — From the contexts: hard, sharp edges at every separation; deep detail; no "film of water" [S18]; density and "muscle quality" [S17]. King Kamali: "deep, deep, deep crispy conditioning" [S14]. Dennis Wolf: "so crispy crisp so dry" [S15].
3. **Confused neighbors** — The older form *crisp*: Phil Heath's "cuts and crispness" [S9], Brandon Curry's "crisp conditioning" [S21], and "a dry, crisp look that judges have asked for" (2026 Olympia report) [S22]. Also *dry* (fullness file), *grainy*, *hard*, *peeled*.
4. **Consistency of usage** — Overlapping slang. Consistent as praise, vague as a cue. Named users: Dallas McCarver's caption "crispy.. extra crispy" (quoted 2016) [S20]; Brandon Curry, "looking forward to being crispy again" (2019) [S19]; Phil Heath (2022) [S17]; Samir Bannout, "come in crispy like 24-carat" (2023) [S16]; Hany Rambod (2024) [S13]. Fans: "next level crispy conditioning" [S34]. **Origin:** the earliest dated example I found is from 2016 [S20]. The word is probably the "-y" form of *crisp* plus a fried-food pun ("extra crispy") [INFERENCE]. I did not find its first use.
5. **Causes: supported vs. speculative** — *Community claims:* water under the skin ("film of water") [S18]; meals eaten during a long show day (Rambod) [S13]; an ethnic "Persian conditioning" (Kamali) [S14], for which I found no evidence. *Evidence:* none direct. Water and sodium practices are not shown to be effective or safe [S35]. One n=1 case shows that subcutaneous thickness can change within days [S52]. Low SAT is the supported base [S37].
6. **Honest illustrative representation** — Treat it as a "sharpness" preset, not a mechanism: a high conditioning value, narrow light-to-shadow transitions at the borders, a light sheen, and an overhead key light. Tag it as slang and as partly presentation. Best regions: abs, quads, delts, back.
7. **Classification** — Overlapping slang; visual description; contested explanation.

### 9. Grainy

1. **Meaning** — A fine, sand-like or stone-like texture across the muscles at extreme condition. OCB makes it a criterion: "An overall grainy look throughout the physique should be seen" [S6]. NPC lists "grainy" as an industry term [S2].
2. **Visible features** — Many small ridges and fine striations over the muscle bellies, and "blocky" abs. It is often seen in back poses (lats, teres, lower back), glutes, and hamstrings. BarBend: "grainy conditioning – a look coined by six-time Mr. Olympia Dorian Yates – with blocky, defined abdominals and striations across his torso" [S11]. Muscle & Fitness on Rich Gaspari: "grainy muscle" with "striated glutes" [S9].
3. **Confused neighbors** — *Striated* and *shredded* (lines, not texture). *Dry* (fullness file). *Mature* muscle (maturity file). *Hard*, *dense*.
4. **Consistency of usage** — Mostly consistent as a texture term among judges and coaches. Its border with *dry* and *mature* is unclear. The "coined by Yates" claim comes from one publication; I did not verify it elsewhere.
5. **Causes: supported vs. speculative** — *No direct evidence found.* *Plausible:* very low SAT [S47], large muscles, and the surface relief of fiber bundles under raking light [S59][S60] [INFERENCE]. *Speculative:* water-based explanations; evidence for water practices is lacking [S35].
6. **Honest illustrative representation** — Add fine, very low surface detail that follows fiber direction, only above a high conditioning value. Make its strength depend on the light angle (stronger under top or side light). Best regions: the rear double biceps view (lats, teres, lower back), glutes and hamstrings, delts.
7. **Classification** — Visual description; overlapping slang; contested explanation.

### 10. Stage-ready (contest-ready)

1. **Meaning** — Fit to compete in a given division on show day, with condition, fullness, tan, and posing all ready. It is division-relative: stage-ready for Bikini is far below stage-ready for bodybuilding [S2][S5]. Coverage also says "contest-ready" [S29], and writers speak of "stage weight" [S25].
2. **Visible features** — The division target from the rule text (A2 table), plus an even stage tan, a light sheen, and practiced posing [S1][S2][S6][S8].
3. **Confused neighbors** — *In shape*; *peaked* / *peak week* (fullness file); "photo-ready".
4. **Consistency of usage** — Mostly consistent as a goal state. The visual target changes with the division and the federation.
5. **Causes: supported vs. speculative** — *Supported:* months of fat loss to contest levels [S37]; tan and posing follow written rules [S1][S2]. *Speculative:* final-week water and carbohydrate methods (fullness file; weak evidence [S35]).
6. **Honest illustrative representation** — Offer division presets built from the rule text: Fit Model, Bikini, Figure, Physique, Classic, Bodybuilding. Each preset sets body cues only. Keep tan, sheen, and stage light on a separate, labeled "stage presentation" toggle.
7. **Classification** — Distinct concept (goal state); division-relative.

### 11. In shape

1. **Meaning** — In general speech, physically fit. In bodybuilding talk it often means contest condition. Flex Wheeler: "we were just in better shape … We were more peeled" [S27]. NPC Fit Model rules use it for a lower level: a "core that is in shape but not too conditioned or lean" [S2].
2. **Visible features** — They vary with the speaker: from a flat, lightly defined core [S2] to full contest detail [S27].
3. **Confused neighbors** — *Conditioned*, *stage-ready*, *lean*, "in condition".
4. **Consistency of usage** — Disputed / overlapping slang. The level depends on the context [S2][S27].
5. **Causes: supported vs. speculative** — The same as *lean* and *conditioning*. No special mechanism is claimed.
6. **Honest illustrative representation** — Do not give it its own control. Show it as a label that can sit at different points of the scale, with the two quotes above to show the range.
7. **Classification** — Overlapping slang.

### 12. Off-season / soft

1. **Meaning** — *Off-season* is the growth phase between contests. A review gives its goal as "to increase muscle mass without adding unnecessary body fat" [S53]. *Soft* means blurred, not hard, muscle borders. People blame fat or water for it (see fullness file).
2. **Visible features** — A rounder outline; shallow or no grooves; less clear abs; fewer veins; a smooth surface; often a larger size. WNBF uses "soft" as a fault in Figure ("should not have a 'soft' look") and as a goal in Bikini ("softer than Figure but lean") [S5]. Muscle & Fitness on Samir Bannout: "too frequently smooth" [S9].
3. **Confused neighbors** — *Smooth*, *watery*, *holding water* (fullness file), *bulking*, *flat*.
4. **Consistency of usage** — *Off-season:* consistent (a phase). *Soft:* overlapping slang; the cause (fat or water) is disputed.
5. **Causes: supported vs. speculative** — *Supported:* drug-free men returned to about 14–15% body fat after contest (14.6% [S38]; 13.8–14.9% [S40]); in some people SAT rises fast (+62% with a 1.4 kg gain in a gymnast) [S47]. *Not universal:* one enhanced Open pro had almost the same skinfolds in the off-season and 10 days before a show [S51]. *Contested:* water as the cause of "soft" (fullness file).
6. **Honest illustrative representation** — Thicken the cover layer so it fills the grooves and softens the edges, and allow a modest size increase. Do not draw a separate "water" effect. Label the effect as schematic.
7. **Classification** — *Off-season:* distinct concept (phase). *Soft:* visual description; contested explanation.

## Open questions / uncertainty

- No study links measured SAT thickness to judged or rated definition in bodybuilders. The fat-to-grooves link rests on surgery papers and rule texts.
- The physical cause of visible striations, cross striations, and "grainy" texture is unknown. The fiber-bundle explanation is my inference.
- Skin thickness in bodybuilders has not been measured, as far as I found. Whether it changes during prep, or explains the "peeled" look, is open.
- Tan, oil, and stage light: the rules and vision science agree in direction, but no experiment tests their effect on perceived conditioning.
- Body-fat data come from small drug-free samples. Method error is large at extreme leanness [S40][S46]. Enhanced Open pros may differ; one case shows almost no skinfold change between off-season and pre-contest [S51].
- The first use of "crispy" is unknown. The earliest dated example found is from 2016, and its spread through social media is not documented.
- No study validates an ordinal mapping of the slang words. Writers use the top-end words interchangeably.
- Claims about time of day, meals, and "a film of water" [S13][S18] are untested.
- The vision-science findings come from simple lab images, not from bodies on a stage.
